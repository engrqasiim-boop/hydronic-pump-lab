Calculation method (SI basis) & design notes

Q = load / (ρ × cp × ΔT) in m³/s with load in kW and cp in kJ/kg·K.
v = Qsection / A; Re = ρvD/μ
1/√f = −2 log₁₀[ε/(3.7D) + 2.51/(Re√f)] for turbulent flow; f = 64/Re for laminar flow.
hpipe = f(L/D)v²/(2g); hfittings = ΣK·v²/(2g)
hequipment = Δp/(ρg) with Δp in Pa.
H = (Σhloss + Hstatic) × (1 + allowance)
Phyd = ρgQH/1000 = 6.30 kW
Pshaft = Phyd/ηpump = 8.40 kW
Pelectric = Pshaft/(ηmotor × ηdrive) = 9.41 kW
Q in m³/s; H in m; efficiencies as fractions; g = 9.80665 m/s². Motor sizing basis: 9.24 kW shaft including reserve.

Darcy friction factor: 64/Re for laminar flow; iterative Colebrook–White equation for turbulent flow. Between Re = 2,300 and 4,000, a linear interpolation between endpoint friction factors is used for visualization only; transition-flow results require engineering review.

Imperial uses US gallons and mechanical horsepower (1 hp = 0.7457 kW); TR = 3.51685 kW. Temperature differences use 1 K = 1.8°F, without a 32° offset. Energy and tariffs remain in kWh and OMR. Calculations are performed in SI internally; toggling units preserves unrounded input values. Motor candidate is the next listed IEC-style rating above shaft power plus reserve. Verify manufacturer absorbed power over the entire operating range, runout, ambient derating, NPSH available/required, minimum flow, pressure rating and duty/standby arrangement before selection.

Closed-loop building height affects fill pressure and component pressure ratings, but not circulating pump differential head. For open towers use basin water level to discharge elevation, plus specified inlet/nozzle pressure. Do not count the same tower head twice.

Condenser-water heat rejection equals cooling duty plus compressor heat; it is not simply the evaporator load. Pressure drops must correspond to each component’s actual design flow.

Scope: steady incompressible flow through a fixed index circuit. For an open system, add any terminal velocity head or boundary pressure difference not already included in nozzle / equipment losses. Full-pipe flow is assumed; tower siphon behavior and partial-fill transients need separate review.

Friction audit: Hydraulic Institute — Darcy–Weisbach and friction factors. K is dimensionless and uses the local section velocity. Power uses total pump flow, not branch flow.

References: Bell & Gossett — Cooling Tower Pumping and Piping · KSB — Pump efficiency. Example inputs are illustrative, not equipment selections or code requirements.

Model equations, assumptions & how to read the curves

SI model: H₀ = 26.77 m; a = 0.005950 m/(L/s)². H_pump(Q,s) = s²H₀ − aQ². H_system(Q) = H_base(Q) + 0.00 × (Q/30.00)² m. The operating point is solved by bisection. Selection allowance is excluded from H_base. Physical constants and conversions retain full precision.

  • The head curve is a quadratic fit through the reference duty and shutoff head. Reference flow is assumed to be the best efficiency point (BEP).
  • Speed ratio s = n/n₀. At homologous points Q ∝ s, H ∝ s² and shaft power ∝ s³ with unchanged efficiency. The actual system operating flow is solved again; it is not assumed proportional to speed when static head is present.
  • Illustrative efficiency: η = ηpeak × x(2 − x), where x = Q/(s × QBEP). It is used only for 0.4 ≤ x ≤ 1.6. Outside that range power and efficiency are deliberately unavailable. A spinning pump at shutoff consumes power; zero hydraulic output does not mean zero motor input.
  • Passive valve and equipment losses scale with Q². Active PICV regulation, variable branch redistribution, cavitation, NPSH, water hammer and startup transients are not simulated.
  • Animation is schematic and slowed for clarity. Particle speed indicates relative flow, not physical transit time. Pump differential pressure is shown; absolute suction and discharge pressures require separate fill-pressure / basin-level data.
